I have [written previously](/2026-11-04-how-easy-is-it-to-build-an-app-now.html) about my todo app. It was fun building and I am not afraid of any code generation any more (at least, front end side). But what I have now on my mind is the question.

> Why build?

Because the fact that you can do something does not mean you should.

You can still use the apps you were already paying for and still get value[^paid].

You can (*at least on macOS*) use the OS API to create features that look almost as if they were part of another app. I mean not only Shortcuts but also Accessibility API that allows show stuff over a window.

You can somehow simplify your workflow and not build anything at all and use in-situ resources (apps) instead of just adding more into your workflow.

If there's no **why** in your build, then why should anyone use it? Pretty obvious, that one probably will not use it. Especially now when almost any of us could build an app that previously would take y*eeee*ars to build.

Take my Things 3 clone. I also replicated Bear and iA Writer. It took me weeks to build. I built my clones because I thought I would gain value from having an app that works *the way I want it to work*[^obsidian].

But it was kind of procrastination that looked like doing something. It also given zero value to the world out side. Sharing would be useless: you could get the same thing in literally any other markdown or todo app.

So why build? What would be the value in your build?

Several months ago I watched a video on Peter Thiel's philosophy and his zero to one idea. I used to think it was about customers, but it appears to be about value and progress and ideas. So for him at that time a founder was someone who creates value: something from nothing, one from zero.

Use your tokens to add value to the world.

[^paid]: Or paid. I prefer one-time purchase apps like iA Writer or Things.
[^obsidian]: In fact it is worse than setting up Obsidian. With Obsidian you don't need $100 subscription.
